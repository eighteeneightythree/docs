Generally, supratherapeutic warfarin dosing in low risk patients without bleeding does not require active reversal. Warfarin should be withheld until the target INR is restored.

Vitamin K should be considered for patients with excessively raised INR or in the groups discussed below in addition to replacement or the inactivated factors.

In clinically significant bleeding or in high risk patients, use of Prothrombinex is reasonable. It is also indicated if reversal is required for planned invasive procedures such as surgery.

> [!warning] NZ Blood has moved from Prothrombinex to Beriplex
> The dosages are much the same...

In life-threatening or critical organ haemorrhage, [[Intravenous fluids#FFP / Fresh Frozen Plasma|FFP]] should be given.

# Mechanism

Warfarin inhibits vitamin K epoxide reductase (VKOR), and to a lesser extent vitamin K reductase, arresting the normal vitamin K cycle. The subsequent reduction in active vitamin K limits gamma-carboxylation of the vitamin K dependent factors.

The form of vitamin K (phytomenadione) used for warfarin reversal is the parent compound and requires a single activation step which is catalysed by an unidentified warfarin resistant quinone reductase.
